Chapter 19

War's End

Guam · August–September 1945

August began with parties at the officers’ club, details, and practice flights with new crews. On the 6th he logged his thirtieth mission. Three days later the radio carried news of the atomic bomb; Russia had declared war on Japan; he had been “over here also last nite.” By the 13th the field was standing by to see if the war was over. Their last mission, he wrote, had been Friday the 11th.

Guam · 9 August 1945 · 31 missions
Dear folks, What's everyone? Suppose you're all excited about the new Atomic Bomb! We were over here also last nite. And Russia has declared war on the Japs also. Looks like the presence of this new bomb there no effect on our missions tho. […] This should shorten The war lots. […] Pardon the scribbling, Guess we're just kinda mad because they don't go ahead & use more Atomic Bombs instead of sending us out again. Love to All Austin
Guam · 13 August 1945 · 32 missions
Dear Mother & Sis, How's everything today? Over here we're just standing by to see if the War is over. We went on our last mission Friday the 11th. If it's still on tomorrow we'll start bombing again I suppose. Everyone over here is sure hoping its over. […] Love to all, Austin
Historical note — August 1945

An atomic bomb was dropped on Hiroshima on 6 August 1945 and another on Nagasaki on 9 August. The Soviet Union declared war on Japan on 8–9 August and invaded Manchuria. Austin’s letters register those headlines as he heard them on Guam — without technical detail he did not have. XXI Bomber Command missions continued until Japan’s acceptance of surrender terms.

On 15 August the letter needed only two words at the top of the thought: War’s over. Happiness on Guam matched what he imagined at home; how soon anyone would leave was still unknown. Mail caught up — ten letters in a morning — and the necklace and Air Medal he had mailed were acknowledged from Cherry. Shows, swimming, rain, and waiting filled the rest of August while the surrender documents were prepared.

Guam · 15 August 1945
Dear Mother and Sis, War's over! And what a happy bunch of people over here on Guam. I suppose the people in the states are just as happy. Don't know exactly what the deal will do for coming home but whatever it is I'll probably be among the 1st bunch to get back from Guam. I got 32 missions in and I think the ones over 30 missions will get back first. However transportation will be another thing. And we might come back by boat. Which would take a month I suppose. Our planes were out bombing again last nite. We were scheduled also but were taken off at the last minute. […] Love to all, Austin
Historical note — surrender

Emperor Hirohito’s recorded surrender broadcast reached the Japanese public on 15 August 1945 (14 August in the United States). The formal surrender was signed aboard USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay on 2 September. Between those dates, Marianas crews lived in a limbo of canceled combat, rumors, and slow shipping and flying schedules home.

September was rain, mud “as the Miss. bottom gumbo,” restlessness, and flying-safety lectures. Capt. Handwerker left for home early in the month. Austin tried to work the complicated process of getting back to the States, mailed small things ahead, and chased rumors of morning processing. On the 27th he was on ATC orders — almost sure to leave by plane. On the 30th he was back in the United States.

Guam · 27 September 1945
DEAR FOLKS, CAN YOU IMAGINE THEM STILL KEEPING ME HERE ON GUAM ALL THIS LONG? I HAVE BEEN PUT ON ATC ORDERS THO SO THAT MEANS THAT I AM ALMOST SURE TO LEAVE HERE BY PLANE. THE SOONER THE BETTER. […] YOU SAID YOU HADN'T HEARD WHERE HANDWERKER HAD GOTTEN HOME YET. WELL HE ISN'T. WE HEARD TODAY THAT HE IS STILL ON SAIPAN WAITING FOR A BOAT. SO MABY I'LL GET HOME FIRST AFTER ALL. […] I'LL CALL YOU WHEN I GET TO SAN FRANCISCO. LOVE TO ALL, AUSTIN
United States · 30 September 1945
Dear Folks, I'm back in the good ole U.S.A. Came by plane from Guam. Sent you a telegram this morning instead of telephoning as it takes so long to make connections by phone. Well here's the dope. I reported to the Port of Embarkation (sp ?) at Hamilton Field, Calif. They will have our orders ready Oct. 4th sending me to some Redistribution Center in Western Ark. I don't remember the name even. So they said we didn't have anything to do until the 4th so we came into San Francisco for the Nite. I'll get a leave from this camp in ARK So I'll probably be around 10 days before I get home. […] Love, Austin
Historical note — ATC and the flight home

ATC was the Air Transport Command, which moved passengers and cargo worldwide. Combat crews going home by air often traveled on ATC orders rather than waiting for a ship. Austin’s September letters show the wait first — rain, rumors, Handwerker leaving ahead of him — then the sudden shift to stateside paper once the airplane landed in the United States.
