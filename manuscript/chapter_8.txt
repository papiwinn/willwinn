Chapter 8
Back to the States Again
September–October 1945

With the war ended, the long wait for transportation home began. Austin remained on Guam through most of September, living in temporary quarters, reading, writing letters, and listening to rumors about shipping dates. Some men left by ship; others, including Austin, eventually secured seats on aircraft heading east.
In late September he flew back across the Pacific and landed in California. A telegram went home instead of a telephone call—connections were still difficult. Processing at Hamilton Field followed. Orders would send him to a redistribution center, then on leave, and eventually toward discharge.
The final letters are filled with relief, impatience, and quiet joy. He asked about the family, the farm, the crops, and the small details of ordinary life that he had missed for two and a half years. The young man who had written his first letter on the Dixieland in January 1943 was coming home an officer, a combat pilot, and a veteran of the air war against Japan.
Home had remained the fixed point through every base, every training phase, and every mission. Now the motion was finally carrying him back toward it.
